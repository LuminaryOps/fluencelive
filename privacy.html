Privacy

Your work stays yours.

Fluence runs on your machine. Here is exactly what it, the Fluence Scan phone app, and this website send, to whom, and why.

Last updated November 23, 2026

No accounts, no tracking

Neither app has analytics, ads, or a sign-in. Your compositions, media, and settings never leave your computer.

Three small requests

Fluence goes online only to check your license, look for updates, and send a crash report when you choose to.

Scan photos stay local

Fluence Scan sends its photos straight to Fluence over your own network. They never touch the internet.

Nothing sold

We don't sell or share personal information, and we don't use it for advertising.

Who we are

Fluence and Fluence Scan are made by Luminary Ops ("we"). Questions about this policy or your information go to admin@fluencelive.com.

The Fluence app

Fluence is desktop software for Windows and macOS. Everything you make in it, including compositions, media, shaders, presets, bindings, and settings, is stored on your computer and is never uploaded. Fluence contacts the internet in only these cases:

License activation and checks

When you activate a license, Fluence sends your license key and your computer's name to our license provider, Lemon Squeezy. The computer name is there so you can recognize each activation in your account. After that, Fluence re-checks the license at most once a day, sending the license key and the activation's ID. Lemon Squeezy is also the merchant of record for purchases (see This website) and handles this information under its own privacy policy.

Update checks

When Fluence starts, it asks GitHub whether a newer release exists, at most once every six hours. This is an ordinary request for public release information. It contains no license or personal details, though GitHub, like any server, sees the request's IP address. If you choose to install an update, the installer is downloaded from GitHub.

Crash reports

If Fluence closes unexpectedly, it offers to send a crash report. Nothing is sent unless you click Send report. A report contains:

  • the Fluence version, your operating system and CPU type, and the time of the crash;
  • the error Fluence recorded and the end of its session log;
  • a crash dump (Windows) or the system crash report (macOS);
  • any note you type in the report window.

Logs and crash dumps can include file paths, which may contain your user name or project names. The crash window shows what will be sent before you send it. Reports go to our own server and are delivered by email to us, used only to find and fix the problem, and deleted when they are no longer needed. The server uses your IP address only to limit how often reports can be sent and does not keep it.

Things you connect yourself

Some features connect to places you choose: web pages you load as clips, NDI and other network video sources and outputs, MIDI and OSC devices, and plugins you install. Those connections go where you point them and are governed by those services and devices, not by us. The Control API that scripts and AI agents use to drive Fluence listens only on your own computer.

Fluence Scan for iPhone and Android

Fluence Scan turns your phone into the camera for Fluence's Surface Scan. It has no account, no analytics, and no ads, and it does not use the internet.

  • Camera. The app uses the camera to read the pairing code that Fluence shows, and to photograph the patterns your projector displays during a scan.
  • Local network. The photos, the scan steps, and your phone's name (so Fluence can show which phone is connected) travel directly between the phone and the computer running Fluence, over your own network. They are not sent anywhere else.
  • Nothing kept. The app does not save photos to your phone or your photo library, and it does not store anything once you close it. The scan result lives in Fluence, on your computer.

The camera and local network permissions can be turned off at any time in your phone's settings. Scanning won't work without them. If you install the app from the App Store or Google Play, those stores handle your download and any data they collect under their own policies. Help with the app is on the Fluence Scan support page.

This website

fluencelive.com has no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking cookies. It is served by Cloudflare, which processes visitors' IP addresses to deliver and protect the site. Its fonts load from Google Fonts, so your browser requests them from Google.

The download page asks GitHub for the latest release so the download buttons point at the newest version. Purchases happen in a checkout run by Lemon Squeezy, our merchant of record, which collects your name, email, billing details, and payment information to process the order and send your license key. We receive your order details from Lemon Squeezy, including your name, email address, and country, and use them only to provide your license and support. We never see your full payment card details.

If you email us

When you write to admin@fluencelive.com, we keep the conversation so we can help you and refer back to it. We don't add you to any mailing list.

Your choices and rights

You can ask us what information we hold about you, and ask us to correct or delete it, by emailing admin@fluencelive.com. Depending on where you live, you may have further rights under laws such as the GDPR or the CCPA, and we will honor them. Purchase records held by Lemon Squeezy can also be managed through Lemon Squeezy directly.

Children

Fluence and Fluence Scan are tools for creative professionals and are not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect information from children.

Changes to this policy

If what the apps or this site send changes, we will update this page and the date at the top.
